**Selected Facts:**

**From Russian (ru):**
1. In the 8th century, Arabs who conquered the Iberian Peninsula began cultivating rice, a staple of their diet.
2. By the beginning of the 19th century, paella was already loved throughout Spain.
3. The word "Paella" comes from the Valencian paella, meaning "pan."
4. In Valencia, the word “paella” refers to all types of pans, including the specific one used to cook the dish, while in most of Spain and Latin America, “paellera” is more commonly used—though the Royal Spanish Academy considers both terms equally valid.
5. Paella is made from rice dyed with saffron, with the addition of olive oil.

**From French (fr):**
1. Saffron has been used for millennia for its culinary, medicinal, and dyeing properties.
2. The starch in the rice promotes the absorption of the broth during cooking.
3. The crust formed at the bottom of the paella dish is a sign of a successful paella.
4. The absorption of the broth allows the flavors of the cooked dish to be transmitted.
5. The paella was eaten straight from the cooking pan.

**From Chinese (zh):**
1. Spanish paella is the food of the Falles festival.
2. The dish was also introduced to the Philippines, a former colony, where it evolved to include local ingredients such as crab, abalone, soy sauce, clams, and annatto (a natural food coloring).
3. Traditional Valencia paella is made with rabbit meat.
