**Selected Facts:**
1. In modern Russian cuisine, Wiener schnitzel is also suggested to be made from pork loin.
2. In Vienna, the name "Viennese schnitzel" only became firmly established at the beginning of the 20th century with the publication of Maria von Rokitansky's culinary work "Austrian Cuisine."
3. In Austria, Wiener schnitzel is traditionally served with a semi-liquid potato salad dressed with vegetable oil and vinegar.
4. Figlmüller restaurant near St. Stephen's Cathedral has been upholding the tradition of its signature pork schnitzel with a diameter of up to 30 cm, fried in vegetable oil, for more than 115 years.
5. In Serbia, there is a rolled variant with traditional regional cheese (Kajmak) and ham.
6. It was presented at the Exposition Universelle in Paris in 1889.
7. A vegan version of schnitzel exists in Israel. The Israeli schnitzel is fried in vegetable oil.
8. Although the dish dates back to 15th–16th century Vienna, the term “Wiener Schnitzel” first appeared in an 1831 Prague cookbook.
9. The Wiener schnitzel was brought to Italy and then to Austria during the Napoleonic wars.
10. The Vienna schnitzel is very similar to the Milanese cutlet from northern Italy.
11. In Shanghai, China, there is a type of Western cuisine similar to Vienna schnitzel.
12. In Japan, the frying method of French fried beefsteak was combined with the marinating method of Viennese schnitzel to create the Japanese-style pork cutlet (tonkatsu).
13. Shanghai fried pork chop is often served with rib and rice cake.
